“Pan” means all.

The name carries the message. Whatever your dis-ease, you can talk about it in this room. The meeting stays focused on the solution, not the problem.

Where Pan came from

In 1999 a group of alcoholics began getting together on retreat, every six months or so, to study and review the Twelve Steps of Alcoholics Anonymous together. Word got around, and some of the group who had “other problems” asked whether people without an alcohol problem could come and take part. It was decided that they could. The Steps address all of them, and it became obvious that the program was never really about the substance or behavior.

At the end of one of those retreats we realized there was no meeting for all of us to attend together, to keep the momentum the retreat had started. So someone suggested we start one, and it was agreed the meeting would focus on working the solution, which we consider the Steps to be, and not on any “problem.”

The first Pan meeting was held on Friday, April 22, 2005. For almost fifteen years it met in person, week after week; when the pandemic came in 2020 it moved onto Zoom, and it’s carried on there ever since. It still meets every Friday. A second meeting, the Pan Twelve Step Workshop, was later added on Mondays for members who wanted to study the Steps themselves rather than only hear them shared.

If you belong to any other Twelve Step program, you’re welcome through our doors too. Pan doesn’t compete with a fellowship; it sits alongside them.

One member’s list

The value of Pan fellowship for me

  1. Focus on the emotional stuff rather than the substance or behavior. Putting down the substance or behavior, for me, is of limited value, because the pain underneath it rises to the surface without the medication. What I want and need is an approach that treats that stuff, which Pan does.
  2. Focus on the fourth column, my part in my disturbance. Understanding that whenever I am disturbed, no matter what the cause, there is something in me that needs to be healed. This lets me take responsibility for my part and so let go of the disturbance, rather than staying in a victim space of anger, fear, or ego.
  3. Closely linked, the Step Ten process. Nobody ever has to leave here with a disturbance. It works, and it makes my life palatable as I work the Steps and find serenity. Pan people can have that from day one, even if their Step Four takes years, if they work the process.
  4. Strong recovery in the meeting. The focus is on the message of strength and recovery rather than being in the problem. Valuable for two reasons: seeing others’ recovery gives me hope and inspiration, and positivity begets positivity (and the reverse). The value of being in a room of miserable people is limited.
  5. Strong community. Encouragement to meet new people, reach out to newcomers, and share Step Tens. It means I never have to deal with a problem alone, and I have many opportunities to do service and get out of my own head. Lots of phone calls during the week; recovery takes place 24/7, not just during the meeting.
  6. Cross-addiction affiliation. I define my illness by my addictive personality rather than my addictions. If they went away and I didn’t work the Steps, other addictions would come along and replace them. It could be any substance or any behavior that would medicate the pain in the short term. (My core addiction is fantasy, which doesn’t have its own program.)
  7. Very active focus on working the Steps. Step study workshop on Monday; the initial speaker on Friday asked to focus on step work; Step Tens shared at both. The Steps are the solution.
  8. Active sponsorship, through sponsors and the wider community. Support for everyone in working the Steps.

What makes Pan different

Two things.

Every dis-ease is speakable

Most rooms are organized around one substance or one behavior. Pan is organized around the Steps. That means you never have to translate your problem into somebody else’s language to be understood here.

Focused on the solution

Shares here are about what worked, not a recital of what went wrong. The suffering is real and it’s heard, but the room’s attention stays on the solution.

How Pan is run

Pan is organized around the Twelve Steps, not around any one substance or behavior. That’s its structure and why the fellowship is relevant for every dis-ease. We are members of many Fellowships and of none. What we share is not a common problem but a common Solution, the Steps lived as a way of life, and a desire for the freedom they bring.

Pan is fully self-supporting through its own members’ contributions. There are no dues and no fees, no outside funding, and nothing that must be bought to belong. In this, as in everything, we follow the example the older Fellowships have already proven: the meeting keeps its own affairs, declines outside support, and keeps any literature or trading plainly separate from the Fellowship’s Seventh-Tradition giving.

We keep no officers over the group and no authority above it. The decisions that belong to Pan are taken by the group as a whole, an informed group conscience, and the Fellowship is kept deliberately simple, so that service stays close to the meeting and never grows into a structure of its own. Pan holds no opinion on outside issues, so that its name is never drawn into public controversy.

We guard one another’s anonymity. Wherever Pan is seen in public, members are named by a first name and a single initial and no more, and what is said in a meeting stays in the meeting. Anonymity is not secrecy. It puts everyone on the same footing, so a newcomer can speak freely at their first meeting.

Pan exists for one thing: to show, by a living Fellowship in which it is plainly happening, the powerful results the Steps can bring. For us, sobriety is not the same as abstinence. Our definition of sobriety is a peaceful, calm, contented, serene, and well-balanced life of joy.

A Twelve Step fellowship for any addiction or compulsion.
