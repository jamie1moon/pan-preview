“Pan” means all.

The name carries the message. Whatever your dis-ease, it is speakable in this room — and the meeting stays focused on the solution, not the problem.

Where Pan came from

Pan grew out of a retreat where people came to study the Twelve Steps together. Some arrived with alcohol problems. Some arrived with what the rooms politely call “other problems.” The Steps met all of them — and it became obvious that the program was never really about the substance.

The Pan meeting has run on a Friday ever since — in person until the pandemic in 2020, on Zoom from then on — and it still does. A second room was later added on Mondays — the Pan Twelve Step Workshop — for members who wanted to study the Steps themselves rather than only hear them shared.

If you belong to any other Twelve Step program, you are welcome through our doors too. Pan does not compete with a fellowship; it sits alongside them.

What makes Pan different

Two things, and they are the whole of it.

Every dis-ease is speakable

Most rooms are organized around one substance or one behavior. Pan is organized around the Steps. That means you never have to translate your problem into somebody else’s language to be understood here.

Focused on the solution

Shares here are about what worked, not a recital of what went wrong. The suffering is real and it is heard — but the room’s attention stays on the way out.

How Pan is run

Pan is organized around the Twelve Steps, not around any one substance or behavior. That is the whole of its structure, and it is the reason a single room can hold every dis-ease at once. We are members of many Fellowships and of none. What we share is not a common problem but a common Solution — the Steps lived as a way of life — and a desire to find the freedom they bring.

Pan is fully self-supporting through its own members’ contributions. There are no dues and no fees, no outside funding, and nothing that must be bought to belong. In this, as in everything, we follow the example the older Fellowships have already proven: the meeting keeps its own affairs, declines outside support, and keeps any literature or trading plainly separate from the Fellowship’s Seventh-Tradition giving.

We keep no officers over the group and no authority above it. The decisions that belong to Pan are taken by the group as a whole — an informed group conscience — and the Fellowship is kept deliberately simple, so that service stays close to the meeting and never grows into a structure of its own. Pan holds no opinion on outside issues, so that its name is never drawn into public controversy.

We guard one another’s anonymity. Wherever Pan is seen in public, members are named by a first name and a single initial and no more, and what is said in a meeting stays in the meeting. Anonymity here is not secrecy — it is the ground of equality that lets a frightened newcomer speak freely at their very first meeting.

What Pan exists to do is simple, and it is the measure of everything above: to show, by a living Fellowship in which it is plainly happening, the powerful results the Steps can bring. For us, sobriety is not the same as abstinence. Our definition of sobriety is a peaceful, calm, contented, serene, and well-balanced life of joy.
