Why Pan

“Pan” means all.

Whatever your dis-ease, you belong here. Pan grew out of a retreat where people came to study the Twelve Steps together. Some arrived with alcohol problems, some with other problems — and the Steps met all of them. The Friday meeting began in April 2004 and met in person for almost sixteen years, until the pandemic in 2020 moved it onto Zoom — where it has met every week since.

One requirement

A desire to find freedom through a deeper understanding of the Twelve Steps. That’s all. Whatever the addiction or compulsion, you’re in the right room.

No dues, no fees

Pan is fully self-supporting through its own members’ contributions. Nothing to pay to walk in, and nothing to pay to stay.

The Steps as a way of life

Not a program you finish, but a way of living you grow into — one day at a time, with people walking the same road.

The journey

A structured way through — not around — what hurts.

The Twelve Steps are a gradual process that works on the thoughts, the behaviors and the relationships tangled up in addiction. You don’t do it alone, and you don’t do it all at once.

  1. Come as you are

    Start with one meeting. Listen. You are welcome to stay quiet for as long as you need to.

  2. Find a sponsor

    Someone a little further down the road who walks the Steps with you, one to one.

  3. Work the Steps

    A clear, gentle sequence — with workbooks and a sponsor’s guide to lean on.

  4. Live it, and pass it on

    The freedom deepens as you carry it to the next person who is still suffering.

Two rooms every week

A meeting, and a workshop.

Friday is the fellowship meeting — a member shares their experience, strength and hope. Monday is the workshop, where we study the Steps themselves.
